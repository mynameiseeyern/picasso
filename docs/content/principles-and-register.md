# Principles and register

## The three principles, applied to a UI

**Solve problems for people, not processors.** Write about the situation someone
is in, not the system that produced it. Use the words they already use.

**Cool, calm and objective.** State what happened. No alarm, no apology, no
exclamation marks.

**Proactive, not reactive.** Lead with what someone can do next, not with what
the product just did.

> ✗ **Don't** — "Oops! We couldn't load your data. Please try again later."
>
> ✓ **Do** — "Couldn't reach the Sydney probe. Retrying every 30 seconds."

## Register

Same voice, less of it. Brand and product are not two personalities — they are
one personality at two volumes.

> ✗ **Don't** — "Uh oh! Something's not quite right — but don't worry, we're on it."
>
> ✓ **Do** — "MOS dropped below 3.6 on 12 trunks."

**Why it differs.** Brand copy persuades someone who chose to read it. A UI
string informs someone in the middle of a task, often during an incident.

**What stays.** Plain words, active voice, "you" and "yours", and the most
important thing first.

**What goes.** Benefit framing, reassurance, exclamation marks, and explaining
the reader's own domain back to them.

**Who is reading.** Usually an engineer who knows UC and CC better than we do.
Explain our concepts, never theirs.
